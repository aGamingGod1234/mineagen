# PR #439: Measured speech cutoff, and long slow lines kept instead of paid for and thrown away

## A speech cutoff set from real timings

- **Problem:** When OpenAI speech was slow, a line waited up to 30 seconds (the provider's own timeout) before the player got it as text. The first cutoff we shipped, 2 seconds, was a guess. It would have turned most ordinary lines into text.
- **Solution:** We timed ten real speech calls and wrote the rule down before the run: three times the slowest call, rounded up to a whole second. That gives a 13 second default. A line that hits it becomes text for that line only and does not mark the provider as down. The host can still change the value.
- **Result:** Measured with ten real `gpt-4o-mini-tts` calls, one at a time with no retries, on 17 Nov 2026. Successful calls took 1,726 to 3,764 ms to full audio (median 2,361 ms). The failed call ran 4,114 ms before it was cut off at the 20 s limit; the rule uses that as the slowest time. Seven of the nine successful calls took longer than the 2,000 ms guess. The longest wait before a slow line becomes text dropped from 30,000 ms to 13,000 ms. One more real call, with the longest legal line at the slowest speed (280 characters, speed 0.5), took 4,136 ms. In that call, synthesis time did not grow with slower speech; three times it is 12,408 ms, still under 13,000. Eleven calls show typical times, not the slow tail.

## Long lines no longer paid for and discarded

- **Problem:** An agent may say up to 280 characters at speeds down to 0.5, but every stage dropped audio longer than 20 seconds. That covers the speech provider, the voice server and the game add-on. One real call (261 characters at speed 0.88) came back at 19.19 s. The same line in another voice went past 20 s and was thrown away after it had been paid for.
- **Solution:** All stages now share one 60 second limit. In one real call, the longest legal line at the slowest speed returned 38.53 seconds of audio, which fits with about 21 seconds to spare. Audio past 60 s would still be discarded after the call.
- **Result:** Before the fix, 1 of the 10 real calls was paid for and discarded. With a fake provider over the real loopback server, a 48 second line now reaches the game whole (all 4,608,000 bytes); before, it failed with `TTS_RESPONSE_TOO_LARGE`. Measured with one real call on 17 Nov 2026 (about 01:45 +08): 280 characters, voice ballad, speed 0.5 took 4,136 ms to full audio and returned 38.53 s of audio with no error, a pace of 0.069 s per character at speed 1. It is one call in one voice. The nine earlier calls ran 0.061 to 0.086 s per character, so other voices could take about 34 to 48 s; that range is an estimate, and tone effects and the slow tail are unmeasured. Cost: the longest line holds about 5.8 MB per 48 kHz copy instead of 1.9 MB, and only lines that long pay it.
