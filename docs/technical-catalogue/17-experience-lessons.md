# PR #17: Carry lessons from one game into the next

## An agent that never learned from its own deaths

- **Problem:** Each game started with an empty memory, so the agent died the same way game after game. In 22 measured games it died 54 times. 20 of the 21 skeleton deaths came within 30 seconds of it running from the skeleton, and all 10 lava deaths were in the Nether. It could write itself notes, but in those 22 games it wrote none. Nothing else wrote them for it.
- **Solution:** The coordinator now reviews every death and every repeated failure itself, from what it already saw in the last minute, without asking the model. It saves a short lesson for this player. In later games the lesson shows up in the agent's memory only when the same situation comes back, such as a skeleton in view, lava nearby, or no food at low health. A lesson never carries a coordinate or a name, and it carries only between games whose seeds are known and different.
- **Result:** Replaying the 22 recorded games through the review recovered all 54 deaths and wrote 268 lessons from 9 rules. The most widespread were worn tools (22 games), digging into water (21) and pressing on near a hostile (17). The two rules that say what the agent was doing (fleeing, walking into lava) need the body's own report that the action started, which these recordings do not keep, so they did not fire in replay. This is measured by replay, with no model calls. A matching lesson adds about 200 to 470 bytes to a wake (estimated 50 to 120 tokens) and nothing otherwise. In a replay of two recorded skeleton moments, asked 16 times per arm of GPT-6.2 Sol, the agent ran from the skeleton 16 of 16 times without the lesson and 9 of 16 times with it, building cover the other 7. The wording decided it: a vaguer "take cover or close in" version changed none. Both were measured on recorded moments, not live games.
