# PR #17: Carry lessons from one game into the next

## An agent that never learned from its own deaths

- **Problem:** Each game started with an empty memory, so the agent died the same way game after game. In 22 measured games it died 54 times. 20 of the 21 skeleton deaths came within 30 seconds of it running from the skeleton, and all 10 lava deaths were in the Nether. It could write itself notes, but in those 22 games it wrote none. Nothing else wrote them for it.
- **Solution:** The coordinator now reviews every death and every repeated failure itself, from what it already saw in the last minute, without asking the model. It saves a short lesson for this player. In later games the lesson shows up in the agent's memory only when the same situation comes back, such as a skeleton in view, lava nearby, or no food at low health. A lesson never carries a coordinate, and it never loads on the seed that taught it.
- **Result:** Replaying the 22 recorded games through the review recovered all 54 deaths. The review wrote 275 lessons from 11 rules. The most widespread were worn tools (22 games), digging into water (21) and pressing on near a hostile (18). This is measured by replay, with no model calls. A matching lesson adds about 200 to 470 bytes to a wake (estimated 50 to 120 tokens) and nothing otherwise. In a replay of two recorded skeleton moments, asked 16 times per arm of GPT-6.2 Sol, the agent ran from the skeleton in the open 16 of 16 times without the lesson. With the lesson it ran 10 of 16 times and built cover the other 6. This was measured on recorded moments, not live games.
